DFT-Based Computational Material Science & Design

Interdisciplinary Approach to Research Challenges

Interdisciplinary knowledge is crucial for addressing complex research challenges and achieving innovative results. This is particularly true in fields like catalysis discovery, where experimental methods alone may not provide sufficient insight into the underlying electronic reasons for observed phenomena.

Computational methods, such as Density Functional Theory (DFT), offer valuable tools to complement experimental investigations. By analyzing electronic structures and energy landscapes, DFT can elucidate reaction mechanisms, predict properties of materials, and guide the design of new catalysts.

Our group is committed to bridging the gap between theoretical predictions and experimental applications. By combining computational chemistry with experimental techniques, we aim to:

  • Solve Experimental Challenges: Utilize computational methods to address experimental limitations and identify effective solutions.
  • Accelerate Discovery: Predict material properties and reaction mechanisms to guide experimental design and optimize processes.
  • Design Novel Materials: Develop new materials with tailored properties for specific applications, such as energy storage, sensing, and electro/photo catalysis.

Main Research Areas

Gas Storage and Sensing

Investigating the properties of nanoparticles, surfaces, and Metal-Organic Frameworks (MOFs) for gas storage and sensing applications. Understanding molecular interactions and adsorption mechanisms for practical applications.

Defects in Semiconductors

Analyzing the impact of defects on the optoelectronic and vibrational properties of 2D materials. Understanding how defects influence electronic structure and device performance.

Energy Devices

Studying solid-state batteries, inorganic/organic interfaces, surface phenomena, and supercapacitors. Developing computational models for next-generation energy storage and conversion technologies.

Computational Catalysis

Utilizing Density Functional Theory (DFT) to understand catalytic processes at the molecular level. Investigating reaction mechanisms, active sites, and designing novel catalysts for sustainable chemistry.

Machine Learning & Informatics

Spearheading data-driven discovery through active learning, materials informatics, and generative AI frameworks. We develop autonomous platforms to explore chemical spaces and optimize materials search.

Current Research Focus

Computational Material Design

Developing new materials with tailored properties for specific applications in energy storage, sensing, and electro/photo catalysis. Using DFT calculations to predict material properties and guide experimental synthesis of novel compounds.

MOFs and Nanoparticle Studies

Investigating Metal-Organic Frameworks and nanoparticle surfaces for gas storage and sensing applications. Understanding adsorption mechanisms, pore structures, and surface interactions through computational modeling.

2D Materials and Defect Engineering

Studying the electronic, optoelectronic, and vibrational properties of two-dimensional materials. Analyzing how defects and dopants affect material performance for semiconductor and energy applications.

Energy Storage Systems

Computational investigation of solid-state batteries, supercapacitors, and energy conversion devices. Modeling interfaces, surface phenomena, and ion transport mechanisms for improved battery performance.

Machine Learning & Materials Informatics

Integrating materials informatics and generative AI frameworks with DFT to accelerate materials discovery. Our work encompasses advanced feature engineering, automated data generation, and deploying autonomous platforms for efficient materials search and optimization, particularly in small-data environments.
